Frequently asked questions

Quick answers, with the details one link away.

What does “usable dark” mean?

It is true dark that meets your Moon settings. With strict settings, it is moon-free. Relaxed settings can also accept some moonlit time. Compare the same night under different settings.

Why doesn’t the dark window start at sunset?

Twilight continues after sunset. True dark begins when the Sun is 18° below the horizon, at astronomical dusk. Read about twilight.

Which view should I use?

Try River for patterns, Lunations for comparing new-moon periods, and Calendar for choosing particular dates. All three use the same calculations. Explore the views.

Which date does an overnight window belong to?

The date of its evening. Friday’s night includes Saturday morning. Before local noon, Tonight still refers to the previous evening.

Whose time zone am I seeing?

The observing site’s, including daylight saving time. Your device’s time zone does not change the displayed plan. The header clock shows the site’s current time, even while you browse future nights. Sites and time zones.

Does new-moon focus change the available darkness?

Focus changes the presentation. The Moon-height and crescent settings change which time counts as usable. Lunations’ summaries cover its displayed nights, so cropping the view also changes the dates included in its total and weekend comparison.

Why are there two windows—or no usable time?

Moonlight can interrupt true dark, leaving separate windows. Some nights have no true dark; others have the Moon above your chosen threshold throughout it. The total adds every separate usable piece. See a split-window example.

Why do the phase name and new-moon date sometimes differ?

The written phase describes illumination at local midnight. The exact new moon is one instant, which can fall during daylight or on a different civil date from the observing night. Calendar marks the civil date; River marks the associated night. Lunar cycles and new moons.

Does elevation account for hills or trees?

Elevation adjusts the observer position and atmospheric calculation. Atlas assumes a level horizon. A Moon-height threshold can stand in for a simple obstruction, but local terrain and trees need separate consideration. Elevation and horizon assumptions.

Does this predict clouds or observing quality?

The calculations cover sunlight, moonlight and the time accepted by your settings. Check clouds, transparency, light pollution, access and your target’s position separately. “Best weekend” means the most computed usable time within the stated search window.

How accurate are the times?

The model is checked against published reference tables. Real atmospheric conditions and your local horizon can shift observed rise/set times. Other apps may use different event definitions or assumptions. See the measured comparisons and limitations.

Can I use it offline?

Calculations, view changes and these docs keep working after the app has loaded. A fresh offline visit or reload is not currently guaranteed.

Where are my saved sites?

In this browser on this device. They do not automatically sync. Use Manage sites → Browser data to export a backup or move them to another browser. Clearing browser site data removes them. Privacy and browser storage.

What does a shared link include?

Dates, view and planning settings. Your site is omitted unless you explicitly include it; without a site, recipients use their own and may see different results. Included site details are readable by anyone with the link. Sharing and privacy.

How do I use Observing mode?

Choose Observing in More’s Theme group, then enable your device’s red color filter separately. The palette is designed to remain readable through that filter. Filter setup and keyboard help.
