Frequently asked questions

How do astronomical darkness and usable dark differ?

Astronomical darkness is when the Sun’s center is more than 18° below the horizon. Usable dark is the part that meets your Moon settings. By default, the Moon must be below the horizon. You can relax the settings to accept some moonlit time. Compare the same night under different settings.

Which view should I use?

Whichever one you like best! All views use the same calculations. Try River to see how dark periods shift over the months. Lunations lines all the new moons up, and Calendar is pretty traditional. Dark Weekends helps you pick a trip. Explore the views.

Which date does an overnight window belong to?

The date which starts the evening. Friday’s night includes Saturday morning. Before local noon, Tonight still refers to the previous evening.

Whose time zone am I seeing?

The observing site’s, including daylight saving time. Your device’s time zone does not change the displayed plan. The page shows the site’s current time at the top, even while you browse future nights. Sites and time zones.

Why are there sometimes two windows, or no usable time?

Moonlight can split astronomical darkness into separate usable windows. Some nights have no astronomical darkness. On others, your Moon settings may rule out the entire interval. The usable dark total adds the time in each usable window. See a split-window example.

Why do the phase name and new-moon date sometimes differ?

The written phase describes illumination at local midnight. The exact new moon is one instant, which can fall during daylight or on a different civil date from the observing night. Calendar marks the civil date; River marks the associated night. Lunar cycles and new moons.

Does elevation account for hills or trees?

No. Elevation adjusts the observer position and atmospheric calculation. We assume a level horizon. A Moon-height threshold doesn't replace a real horizon with hills and trees, so take it with a grain of salt. Elevation and horizon assumptions.

Does this predict clouds or observing quality?

No. Atlas shows when the sky is dark and how much of that time meets your Moon settings.

How accurate are the times?

The model is computed using Astronomy Engine and checked against published reference tables from U.S. Naval Observatory annual tables. Real atmospheric conditions and your local horizon can shift observed rise/set times. Other apps may use different event definitions or assumptions. See the measured comparisons and limitations.

Can I use it offline?

Yes. Open Atlas while online and check More options for Ready to reopen offline. You can then reload or open the same Atlas address in a new tab in the same browser, even without a connection.

If the browser clears its saved files, open Atlas online again to prepare a new offline copy. When More shows an offline update is ready, close all Atlas tabs and reopen it to use the update.

Can I add observing dates to my calendar?

Choose More → Export to calendar, pick dates and event types, and download the .ics file. In Apple Calendar on Mac, use File → Import. Import into a separate Atlas calendar so you can show or hide it and replace its events when you export again.

The file is a snapshot and can be created offline. It requests all-day events that show as free. Your calendar may apply its own alerts. Atlas links omit your site unless you choose to include it. Anyone with access to the destination calendar may see the exported times.

Where are my saved sites?

In this browser on this device. They do not automatically sync to other devices. Use Manage sites → Browser data to export a backup or move them to another browser. Clearing browser site data removes them. Privacy and browser storage.

What does a shared link include?

Share site opens your location in Around now. It reuses a saved site or saves a new one in the reader’s browser, with Undo. Share plan restores the selected range, view, and Moon settings. Recipients use their own location unless you choose to include yours. Anyone with a link that includes a site can read its details. Sharing and privacy.

How do I use Observing mode?

Choose Observing in More’s Theme group, then enable your device’s red color filter separately. The palette is designed to remain readable through that filter. Filter setup and keyboard help.
