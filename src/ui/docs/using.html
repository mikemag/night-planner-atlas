Choose your site, compare the nights, and open one for its exact windows.

Read a night

Follow the night’s colors from evening to morning. Twilight shades lead into true dark. A Moon-up wash shows where moonlight enters true dark; a green duration bar summarizes your usable time.

Choose what counts as usable

Start with Moon’s upper edge above 0° and Ignore a Moon under: Off (strict) for moon-free darkness. A higher Moon threshold accepts time while the Moon remains below that height. Ignoring a crescent accepts all true dark when the midnight illumination is below your chosen percentage.

Both settings change totals, windows and weekend picks in every view. The drawing still shows moonlight above the natural horizon, with accepted time distinguished from strict moon-free time. See the full definitions and split-window example.

River, Lunations and Calendar

River

River stacks one row per night with clock time across. Year shows the shape of the loaded range; Month gives readable nightly windows. Tap a Month row or press Enter/Space on the chart to expand it. Boundary callouts show Sun/twilight events, Moon events and any additional usable-window boundaries. Tap again or press Escape to collapse. The fixed strip below the chart previews the pointed-at night; tapping or using the keyboard supplies details without hover.

New-moon focus opens the nights within ±N of new moon and compresses the context. Year stays compact. The exact New Moon label identifies one event-associated night, while the written phase name can describe several nights. The label’s local date/time can differ from the row’s evening date.

The desktop minimap navigates the range without changing selection. Tab to Browse River dates for keyboard browsing with arrow keys, Page Up/Down and Home/End. Dark weekends ahead lists the Friday–Saturday pair with the most combined usable time within seven nights of each upcoming new moon.

Calendar

Month shows usable hours, the time window and an inset vertical green duration bar on each date. Split windows remain separate in the night detail. Muted neighboring dates preserve week context; arrow keys navigate real dates in their own month. Scroll up or down to browse months without changing the selected night.

Week fits seven nights on one sunset-to-sunrise clock, with evening at the top, morning below, and time-only labels at shaded boundaries, including where moonlight enters or leaves true dark. A connector appears only when a label moves away from its boundary to avoid crowding. Scroll through weeks as well. Selecting a date inserts the same horizontal event timeline as River after its row, reserving space so it covers no dates.

Year shows the same 14 loaded months, with new-moon dates and daily duration bars; switching views reuses the computed nights. Activate a month to open it. Calendar's new-moon markers use the event's civil date at the site, which can differ from River's evening date. The same planning controls appear in Calendar, River and Lunations.

Lunations

Lunations aligns cycles around new moon to compare seasonal windows and weekends. Evening runs down to morning on a shared clock scale; green bars beneath each cycle show usable hours on a shared duration scale. Select a night to expand its horizontal timeline with exact event times. Each cycle's total explains which evening dates it includes.

Each chart’s toolbar keeps its zoom controls and browsed dates together. On narrower screens, open Legend for the chart colors and their meanings; wider screens also show a compact key inline. New moons are labeled directly in the charts.

Full cycle shows every loaded night exactly once. Each night belongs to the new moon nearest its site-local midnight; an exact tie goes to the earlier new moon. All rows keep new moon centered, with empty edge positions where a night belongs to the adjacent cycle. These are padding, while a partial cycle means some of its dates lie outside the loaded range. Focused ±N windows intentionally show fewer nights.

Lunations starts at ±3 on phones unless you have chosen a focus window or opened a shared plan. Wider windows pan together inside the chart while the clock rail stays in place. Left/Right visit displayed nights and Up/Down move between cycles at the same new-moon offset. Resizing keeps your selection; a selected night outside the displayed window can still be expanded.

Sharing and browser data

Share copies a link to the loaded dates, view, zoom, focus, planning thresholds and selected night. Site inclusion is off by default, so a recipient uses their own site and may see different results. Themes remain a personal preference.

Open More → Share plan to copy a link. Including the site is off by default; review the site fields before choosing to include them. Opening someone else’s link keeps its settings and included site temporary until you deliberately save them.

For backups or a move to another browser, open Manage sites → Browser data. Export sites and preferences to a local file, then review an import before replacing saved data. The previous saved copy can be exported for recovery. You can reset preferences while keeping sites, restore missing starter sites, or clear rebuildable calculations separately.

Theme choices and OS red-filter setup

The Theme group in More offers System, Light, Dark and Observing. Your choice stays in this browser. System follows the device’s appearance; explicit Light and Dark override it. Observing uses warm neutral levels and extra boundary cues designed to remain distinct through a red-only accessibility filter.

Enable the device filter separately. On Mac, open System Settings → Accessibility → Display → Color Filters, choose Color Tint and set a red tint. On iPhone, open Settings → Accessibility → Display & Text Size → Color Filters; choose a color tint and adjust its hue/intensity toward red. See Apple’s Mac filter instructions and iPhone filter instructions.

The planner cannot control OS filters or guarantee dark adaptation. Display brightness, other apps and notifications still affect what reaches your eyes.

The site sets the clock

Every chart time uses the observing site’s IANA time zone, including daylight saving time. Your device’s zone does not change the plan. The clock beside the location shows the site’s current date, time, UTC offset and zone; selecting a future night does not change that clock.

All three views use site-local clock time and mark clock-change nights in their shared night details. A repeated or skipped hour is real; duration totals add actual elapsed time.

A fresh browser starts at Seattle’s Paramount Park, with Edinburgh’s Royal Observatory, Sydney’s Linden Observatory, Chile’s Vera C. Rubin Observatory, Quito’s Astronomical Observatory and Svalbard’s Kjell Henriksen Observatory also available. Compare them to see northern and southern seasons, near-equatorial nights and polar extremes. Your existing saved records and selection are kept when the app updates. These six starters are added once to existing saved lists; you can edit or remove them afterward.

Choose a saved site in the location dropdown to switch immediately. Choose Manage sites… to add or edit favorites. Paste decimal latitude, longitude or a supported full Google Maps search link, check the coordinates shown for a pasted link, name the site and confirm its time zone. Elevation is optional. Save & use applies the complete record; Cancel or Escape leaves the current site alone. Duplicate makes a separate favorite; removal asks for confirmation.

Leave the time zone blank to find it from the coordinates when saving. You can enter an IANA zone to override it. If several zones cover the point, choose one from the suggestions. Errors appear beside the field that needs attention.

Maps links are input text, parsed locally. Short links, named-place-only links and camera positions cannot establish the intended point. For a reliable copy in desktop Google Maps, right-click the point and click its coordinate line. On a phone, hold an unlabeled point to drop a pin; coordinates appear in Android’s search box or the iPhone pin’s place details. Google’s coordinate instructions show the steps. Paste those coordinates into the location field; short links and the map’s center do not identify a reliable observing point. Latitudes from −90° to +90° are supported. Polar charts show the full noon-to-noon period when the Sun does not rise or set. A missing sunrise does not mean uninterrupted true dark: midday twilight can still occur. Darkness throughout the whole period counts its actual elapsed time, including clock changes.

Automatic zones, elevation and what is not modeled

Time zones are inferred locally from bundled geographic boundaries. Simplified borders can be imperfect, and geographic data and your browser’s civil-time rules have different update schedules. Check the visible zone, particularly near borders or disputed areas; manual overrides are supported. Multiple zone results require you to choose.

Elevation accepts −500 to 10,000 metres above sea level. Blank means sea level. It adjusts the observer’s position and idealized refraction using a standard atmosphere. The assumed horizon stays level: elevation is not a terrain profile or a height above nearby obstructions, and no horizon dip is inferred.

No local terrain, clouds, atmospheric transparency, light pollution or target visibility is predicted. Real temperature, pressure and near-horizon refraction can move observed rise/set times.
