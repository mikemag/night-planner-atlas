Read a night

A night carries the date of its evening and runs from local noon to the next local noon. Friday’s row includes Saturday morning. Before noon, Tonight still means the previous evening.

DayCivil NauticalAstronomical True dark

True dark is the interval from astronomical dusk to astronomical dawn, when the Sun’s center is at least 18° below the horizon. The strict moon-free total removes the parts of that interval when any part of the Moon is above the natural horizon.

Plain black marks genuinely moon-free darkness. A warm grey wash shows Moon-up time within true dark; fuller moons make a brighter wash. This illustrates moonlight, rather than predicting calibrated sky brightness. Friday and Saturday nights are emphasized; the selected night has an outline and Tonight has a separate marker.

Why rise/set events and altitude curves use different points

Moonrise is the moment the Moon’s upper edge reaches a level horizon as seen from your site. The calculation includes lunar parallax, a radius that changes with distance and atmospheric refraction. The Moon’s center can still be below 0° when its upper edge appears.

The inspector’s curves and River’s pointer altitude show the apparent center, sampled every ten minutes. Those samples illustrate position; event times and usable-window boundaries come from separate refined crossing searches.

Natural-horizon Moon events use 34 arcminutes of sea-level refraction. The elevated Moon thresholds use altitude-dependent refraction; elevation scales refraction with air density.

Sunrise and sunset use a Sun-center altitude of −0.833° at sea level, representing the conventional visible-disk edge with standard refraction. Twilight uses the geometric Sun center at −6°, −12° and −18°. See the USNO definitions of rise, set and twilight.

Choose what counts as usable

Start with strict settings: Moon’s upper edge above 0°, with no crescent ignored. Two controls can accept more observing time:

  • Moon’s upper edge above 5°, 10° or 15° treats the Moon as an obstruction only above that apparent height. This can stand in for a simple tree line or ridge.
  • Ignore a moon under 10%, 20% or 30% lit accepts the whole true-dark interval on nights below that illumination threshold. Illumination is evaluated at local midnight.

These choices change usable windows, duration totals and weekend picks. They do not remove the Moon from the drawing. Moonlight shading always follows the disk’s upper edge at the natural 0° horizon. Moonlit time accepted by settings has a green wash in Light/Dark, or a dashed boundary in Observing. Its brightness still follows illumination. Truly moon-free time stays black.

A night can have two separate usable windows. The total adds their actual elapsed durations; pieces shorter than one minute are discarded. A missing window is explained, including nights when the Sun never reaches astronomical darkness.

The site sets the clock

Every chart time uses the observing site’s IANA time zone, including daylight saving time. Your device’s zone does not change the plan. The clock beside the location shows the site’s current date, time, UTC offset and zone; selecting a future night does not change that clock.

River uses local clock time across its rows and marks clock-change nights. A repeated or skipped hour is real. The inspector instead plots elapsed time, so a repeated hour does not fold its altitude curves.

Choose a saved site in the location dropdown to switch immediately. Choose Manage sites… to add or edit favorites. Paste decimal latitude, longitude or a supported full Google Maps search link, check the coordinates shown for a pasted link, name the site and confirm its time zone. Elevation is optional. Save & use applies the complete record; Cancel or Escape leaves the current site alone. Duplicate makes a separate favorite; removal asks for confirmation.

Leave the time zone blank to find it from the coordinates when saving. You can enter an IANA zone to override it. If several zones cover the point, choose one from the suggestions. Errors appear beside the field that needs attention.

Maps links are input text, parsed locally. Short links, named-place-only links and camera positions cannot establish the intended point. For a reliable copy in desktop Google Maps, right-click the point and click its coordinate line. On a phone, hold an unlabeled point to drop a pin; coordinates appear in Android’s search box or the iPhone pin’s place details. Google’s coordinate instructions show the steps. Paste those coordinates into the location field; short links and the map’s center do not identify a reliable observing point. Latitude is currently supported from −65° to +65°.

Automatic zones, elevation and what is not modeled

Time zones are inferred locally from bundled geographic boundaries. Simplified borders can be imperfect, and geographic data and your browser’s civil-time rules have different update schedules. Check the visible zone, particularly near borders or disputed areas; manual overrides are supported. Multiple zone results require you to choose.

Elevation accepts −500 to 10,000 metres above sea level. Blank means sea level. It adjusts the observer’s position and idealized refraction using a standard atmosphere. The assumed horizon stays level: elevation is not a terrain profile or a height above nearby obstructions, and no horizon dip is inferred.

No local terrain, clouds, atmospheric transparency, light pollution or target visibility is predicted. Real temperature, pressure and near-horizon refraction can move observed rise/set times.

Use the planner

River stacks one row per night with clock time across. Year shows the shape of the loaded range; Month gives readable nightly windows. Tap a Month row or press Enter/Space on the chart to expand it. Boundary callouts show Sun/twilight events, Moon events and any additional usable-window boundaries. Tap again or press Escape to collapse. The fixed strip below the chart previews the pointed-at night; tapping or using the keyboard supplies details without hover.

New-moon focus opens the nights within ±N of new moon and compresses the context. Year stays compact. The exact New Moon label identifies one event-associated night, while the written phase name can describe several nights. The label’s local date/time can differ from the row’s evening date.

The desktop minimap navigates the range without changing selection. Tab to Browse River dates for keyboard browsing with arrow keys, Page Up/Down and Home/End. Dark weekends ahead lists the Friday–Saturday pair with the most combined usable time within seven nights of each upcoming new moon.

Calendar puts evening dates into month grids, with Time ↓ or Time → and expandable weeks. Lunations aligns cycles around new moon to compare seasonal windows and weekends. These views keep a selected-night inspector with altitude curves and stage times.

Share copies a link to the loaded dates, view, zoom, focus, planning thresholds and selected night. Site inclusion is off by default, so a recipient uses their own site and may see different results. Themes remain a personal preference.

Theme choices and OS red-filter setup

The Theme dropdown offers System, Light, Dark and Observing. Your choice stays in this browser. System follows the device’s appearance; explicit Light and Dark override it. Observing uses warm neutral levels and extra boundary cues designed to remain distinct through a red-only accessibility filter.

Enable the device filter separately. On Mac, open System Settings → Accessibility → Display → Color Filters, choose Color Tint and set a red tint. On iPhone, open Settings → Accessibility → Display & Text Size → Color Filters; choose a color tint and adjust its hue/intensity toward red. See Apple’s Mac filter instructions and iPhone filter instructions.

The planner cannot control OS filters or guarantee dark adaptation. Display brightness, other apps and notifications still affect what reaches your eyes.

Accuracy, with the comparisons attached

The reference suite uses independently published, minute-rounded USNO annual tables for Seattle, Edinburgh and Sydney. The committed cases were verified on October 16, 2026 and include northern/southern seasons, absent twilight, new/full moons and both directions of a daylight-saving change.

The USNO comparison tests allow 60 seconds for rounding and convention/ephemeris differences. These measured cases are not a universal error bound. Real atmospheric refraction can shift observed rise/set by a minute or more, especially at shallow angles. Terrain and weather remain outside the calculation.

Search windows, numerical tolerances and reference provenance

Solar events are searched from local noon for 24 hours. Moon events and Moon-up intervals use local noon +2 h through noon +22 h. Events outside that window are absent, even if they occur on the same civil date. A clipped interval edge is not reported as a rise or set.

Moon crossings are refined at 0°, 5°, 10° and 15°. A ten-minute scan brackets extrema, refined extrema split the altitude curve into monotone arcs, and bisection locates crossings to a 50 ms bracket. This preserves grazing event pairs hidden between samples. A tangent touch adds no Moon-up interval.

The audit checks all 365 nights of 2026 at Seattle, Edinburgh and Sydney at all four heights, using an independent one-minute scan and crossing refinement of the same underlying ephemeris. Synthetic tests cover two-second grazing pairs that a one-minute scan could miss. These isolate numerical search error rather than testing an independent lunar ephemeris.

Elevation checks at −400 m, 2,000 m and 4,500 m compare with Astronomy Engine’s separate level-horizon rise/set search: within one second for the Moon and ten seconds for the Sun. They use the same ephemeris and do not establish independent elevated-site accuracy.

The stored USNO fixture retains exact query URLs, decimal reference coordinates, UTC dates and verbatim table cells. Reference dates are Seattle October 3, 9, 25 and 31; Edinburgh June 20; Sydney October 3 and December 21, all in 2026. UTC tables avoid applying a fixed civil offset across DST. The reference fixture is never regenerated from the planner’s own results.

    Privacy and credits

    Saved sites and preferences are kept in this browser’s local storage. There are no accounts, analytics or remote coordinate lookups. The app loads its static assets from its own origin and calculations keep working after loading when the network is disconnected. Clearing browser site data removes saved favorites and preferences. If storage is unavailable, temporary changes are identified.

    A shared link normally omits the whole site record. Choosing Include this site explicitly adds the reviewed name, coordinates, zone and optional elevation to the URL fragment. Anyone with that link can read them, and the URL can remain in browser history. Opening a link keeps its controls and included site temporary. Saving view settings and saving the site as a favorite are separate deliberate actions. Share links use readable fields after the #, with default settings omitted. The plan is carried in the link itself; there is no link-shortening service. Previously copied links still work.
